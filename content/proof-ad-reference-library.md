## Core Principle

Proof does not need public client names to work.

It needs:

- The type of person or business.
- The problem they were stuck with.
- The exact business moment that changed.
- The number, screenshot, quote, or behaviour that proves it.
- The mechanism that makes the result believable.
- A clear reason why names are withheld.

Anonymous proof fails when it becomes vague. Anonymous proof works when the context is specific.

## What To Steal

### 1. Lead With The Result, Not The Name

From the B2B case-study references: the headline should make the right prospect self-identify fast.

Weak pattern:

- How [client name] worked with us.

Strong pattern:

- How a [client type] achieved [specific outcome] after fixing [specific bottleneck].

Radical Edge version:

- `$10K month from organic leads`
- `The lead was warm before the call`
- `Chosen before the sales call`
- `The viewing came from content`
- `The sale was not the first touchpoint`

### 2. Make The Business Bottleneck Concrete

The best references do not say "we helped them grow." They explain the broken part of the business.

Useful bottlenecks for Radical Edge:

- Content gets views but no consults.
- Prospects like the content but do not know what to buy.
- Founder has attention but no offer bridge.
- Sales calls start from zero trust.
- WhatsApp follow-up has no context.
- The audience knows the face but not the category.
- Posting volume increased, but demand did not.
- Referrals happen randomly instead of from visible authority.

### 3. Treat The Real Buyer Moment As The Hook

Evergreen Growth's useful pattern: use the real email, chat, objection, or buyer action. The moment itself does the work.

For Radical Edge:

- A WhatsApp message about payment can be stronger than a polished testimonial.
- A referral message can be stronger than a client quote.
- A prospect saying they followed the content first is the ad.
- A viewer asking about services after consuming content is the ad.

Do not over-summarise these into generic copy.

Bad:

- `Content creates demand.`

Better:

- `The prospect already had context.`
- `The call did not start cold.`
- `The referral came through content.`
- `The sale was not the first touchpoint.`

### 4. Use Anonymisation As A Trust Signal

RZ Web Media and Propaxio both make anonymisation explicit: clients are real, numbers are real, names are withheld for privacy or competitive reasons.

Radical Edge should use the same logic:

- We do not name clients unless attribution is approved.
- We blur private details before publishing.
- We use the client type, proof context, and result.
- If a serious buyer needs verification, it can be handled privately.

Suggested small disclaimer:

- `Client details withheld. Proof source verified internally. Results vary.`
- `Names and private details redacted. Result context verified before publishing.`
- `Example only. Client identifiers removed. Results vary.`

### 5. Metrics Need Context

Qwestyon and Propaxio do not just show numbers. They attach the number to the business mechanism.

For Radical Edge:

- `$10K month` means less if the ad does not say it came from organic leads.
- `1M views` means less if the ad does not say it came from focused content, not random volume.
- A warm enquiry means less if the ad does not show the prospect had already consumed the content.

Number-only proof can feel fake. Number plus mechanism feels believable.

## Proof Ad Structures

### Structure A: Result Plus Mechanism

Use when proof has a clean number.

- Header: `[specific result] from [specific source]`
- Middle: proof screenshot or redacted proof name
- Bottom: what actually changed in the buyer journey
- CTA: masterclass

Examples:

- `$10K month from organic leads`
- `$5K closed in a day`
- `$20K month from organic demand`
- `219,858 organic views`

### Structure B: Buyer-Moment Proof

Use when proof is a WhatsApp, DM, email, enquiry, referral, or booking.

- Header: The moment, not the abstract benefit.
- Middle: screenshot frame.
- Bottom: why the moment matters.

Examples:

- `The lead was warm before the call`
- `The viewing came from content`
- `The referral came through content`
- `She did not start from cold`

### Structure C: Before-After Bottleneck

Use when proof has a clear stuck point.

- Before: what they were trying.
- Bottleneck: why it did not convert.
- After: what changed.
- Proof: screenshot or number.

Example:

- Before: posting more.
- Bottleneck: weak point of view.
- After: content gave buyers context before the call.

### Structure D: Anonymous Case Snapshot

Use when names cannot be shown but the proof is strong.

- Client type: `Education founder`, `financial adviser`, `real estate agent`, `consultant`.
- Starting point: what was broken.
- Intervention: what changed in content, offer bridge, WhatsApp, or sales call.
- Result: exact public-safe proof.
- Caveat: results vary, private details redacted.

### Structure E: Proof Ping

Use when the ad should feel like a quick signal rather than a case study.

- One line of context.
- One screenshot/proof frame.
- One interpretation.

This works best for retargeting or warm audiences who already understand Radical Edge.

## Radical Edge Copy Rules

Write like a sharp founder/operator.

Use:

- Concrete business pain.
- Short lines.
- Specific mechanisms.
- Plain English.
- Buyer journey language.
- Sales-call reality.
- Proof-first framing.

Avoid:

- Unlock, transform, elevate, skyrocket, ultimate, dominate.
- Secret, hack, effortless, guaranteed, game-changing.
- Generic "build authority" unless the proof actually shows authority turning into a business outcome.
- Generic "get more leads" unless the proof shows lead quality, enquiry, booking, referral, or close.
- Motivational language.
- Over-polished headline phrasing.
- Client names in public ad designs unless approved.

Use these patterns for AI generation.

- `[buyer action] came from content`
- `[sales outcome] before the call`
- `[metric] from focused content`
- `[referral/enquiry/viewing] from trust`
- `[specific proof] not [wrong solution]`
- `[client type] stopped [wrong activity]`
- `[prospect behaviour] changed before sales`

Examples for Radical Edge:

## Visual Rules For Static Proof Ads

The reference set points toward simple, high-signal creative.

Use:

- Large result headline.
- One proof frame.
- Short interpretation.
- Clear CTA.
- Redacted screenshot when trust matters.
- Anonymous proof label when privacy matters.
- Minimal design noise.
- No unnecessary client photo.

Avoid:

- Explaining the entire case study on the image.
- Showing internal labels like `copy-only proof`.
- Showing private client names.
- Filling the card with disclaimers.
- Making screenshots too small to feel real.
- Using a quote when a buyer action is stronger.

## How To Use Confidential Proof

If the proof source includes names, phone numbers, payment details, addresses, private profile photos, or identifying business details:

1. Keep the raw proof internal.
2. Extract the public-safe claim.
3. Redact the screenshot before Canva polish.
4. Use the client type or proof moment instead of the name.
5. Add a small disclaimer.
6. Keep exact proof context in the metadata doc.

Public design should show:

- The result.
- The business moment.
- The proof frame.
- The interpretation.

Internal database should keep:

- Client name.
- Source file.
- Exact claim.
- Timeframe.
- Attribution status.
- Redaction notes.
- Risk notes.

## Radical Edge Default Interpretation Lines

Use these as bottom copy or supporting text.

- `The sale was not the first touchpoint.`
- `The prospect came in with context.`
- `Reach only matters when buyers remember you.`
- `The content gave the buyer a reason to come closer.`
- `The call did not start from zero trust.`
- `The proof is not the revenue. The proof is the buyer behaviour before the revenue.`
- `Posting more was not the fix. The offer bridge was.`

## What This Means For The Website Bot

When generating ads:

1. Pick exactly one proof source per ad.
2. If proof is a conversion screenshot, make the screenshot the centre frame.
3. If proof is a transcript, use it to shape the headline only.
4. Do not put client names in the actual ad.
5. Build the headline from the result, buyer moment, or bottleneck.
6. Build the bottom line from what changed in the buying journey.
7. Never generate generic marketing claims.
8. Flag weak proof instead of stretching it.
9. Keep the output Canva-polishable, not final-public.

## Best Current Radical Edge Ad Formula

For most proof ads:

```text
[SPECIFIC RESULT OR BUYER MOMENT]

[REDACTED SCREENSHOT / PROOF NAME]

[WHAT THIS PROVES ABOUT THE BUYING JOURNEY]

Join the masterclass
```

The ad should feel like evidence from the field, not a polished agency case study.
